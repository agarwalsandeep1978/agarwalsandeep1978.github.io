Mad Engineers #25100

In Grade 10, I was part of the 12-student Mad Engineers team at Oberoi International School. I worked across mechanical assembly, electrical wiring and coding on our 18-inch competition robot for the 2024–25 INTO THE DEEP season, and served as Co-Head Scout.

Building the competition robot

I contributed across the robot build: designing and fabricating parts, assembling mechanisms, wiring electrical components and working on code. The robot had to fit within an 18 × 18 × 18-inch size limit and compete in INTO THE DEEP, an ocean-exploration game in which robots collected and scored samples before attempting an end-game climb.

Working on the robot connected classroom physics to engineering decisions. Gearing and torque influenced mechanism design; forces and motion mattered at each joint; motors brought in electromagnetism, while sensors and actuators depended on electronics, energy and power. I learned how closely mechanical, electrical and software decisions depend on one another in a working system.

Co-Head Scout

Alongside the build, I served as Co-Head Scout. At tournaments, I evaluated other teams’ robots, looking at their strengths, weaknesses and strategies. Since FTC alliance partners can change from match to match, scouting helped our team understand which combinations of robots could work well together and make informed alliance-strategy decisions.

Inspire Award and Houston qualification

At the FTC India National Championship in 2025, our team won the Inspire Award against 48 teams. The Inspire Award recognises a team’s overall embodiment of FTC, considering its robot, engineering portfolio, judge interviews, teamwork and outreach — not match wins alone.

Winning the award qualified Mad Engineers for the FIRST Championship in Houston, USA, held in April 2025. Competing internationally was the culmination of a season of designing, fabricating, wiring, coding, testing and improving one robot as a team.

What I took away

FTC taught me to approach engineering as a team activity in which every part of the system matters. I learned to move between mechanical, electrical and coding tasks, document and test decisions, and use scouting to understand the wider competitive environment. It was also an early experience of how persistence, communication and shared responsibility shape a technical project.
