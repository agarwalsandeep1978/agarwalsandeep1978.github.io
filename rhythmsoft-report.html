Rhythmsoft Robotics & Automation — Internship Learning Report

Five-day job-shadowing internship · Nashik, Maharashtra · Industrial robotics, manufacturing and automation

Internship objective

I wanted to gain first-hand exposure to industrial robotics and automation in a manufacturing environment, understand how engineers develop and deploy robotic solutions for shop-floor applications, and explore the links between mechanical, electrical, electronics and software engineering.

About the facility

Rhythmsoft Robotics and Automation develops turnkey robotics and automation solutions, particularly for automotive manufacturing. The Nashik facility provided a setting to observe how machinery, robotic systems, controls and production processes are integrated into working industrial systems.

Day 1 — Orientation and factory tour

I was introduced to the facility, its automation divisions, safety procedures and the lifecycle of industrial equipment. The tour established how a manufacturing solution moves from a customer's requirements to mechanical design, fabrication, assembly, control and deployment.

Day 2 — Machines and tools

I compared manual milling with more advanced milling and drilling equipment. Manual machines depend heavily on operator control, while advanced machines allow movement across multiple axes and can execute programmed operations. I learnt why roughing and finishing passes are separated, how coolant supports cutting, and why accurate measurement and edge finishing matter. I also observed lathe, tapping and precision measurement processes.

Day 3 — CNC and precision machining

I studied semi-automatic milling and drilling, including how an operator defines a reference origin and programmes cuts. I observed a vertical machining centre and learnt how programmed tool paths, workpiece alignment, coolant and multiple cutting passes support accuracy and repeatability. These observations helped me connect the geometry of a part with the machine settings required to produce it.

Day 4 — KUKA six-axis robot and pneumatic clamping

I explored a KUKA industrial robot, its axes, teaching pendant and control cabinet. I learnt the distinction between training, testing and automatic operating modes, and the importance of safety controls when moving a robot. I also studied pneumatic clamping: compressed air drives a piston to clamp a workpiece securely, while a separate air path releases it. This demonstrated how fixtures and actuators support a robotic process.

Day 5 — Control panels, PLCs and electrical theory

I examined a control panel containing circuit protection, switches, transformers and a programmable logic controller (PLC). The PLC coordinates machine actions based on programmed logic and inputs. I also explored three-phase power, AC and DC, inductive/resistive/capacitive loads and power factor. These concepts helped me understand the electrical infrastructure behind reliable automation.

Key reflections

The internship gave me an end-to-end perspective of a machine: raw material and fabrication, mechanical assembly, robotic motion, electrical controls and deployment on a live shop floor. I saw that automation depends on coordination across disciplines and on careful attention to safety, precision and process design. It also helped me distinguish between designing and building machines and operating them at production scale.
