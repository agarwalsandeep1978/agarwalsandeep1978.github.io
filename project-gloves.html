Maker Profile · Project 05

SmartSense — Sensored Gloves

I designed a sensor-integrated glove to help people with visual impairments detect nearby obstacles through vibration, exploring whether a wearable device could complement the limitations of a traditional walking cane.

Understanding the user

My starting point was the need for a product that could be carried easily, offer a good grip and remain light and comfortable. A cane can be useful but has limitations in detecting obstacles beyond its reach and may not suit every situation. I explored a glove that keeps the sensing device attached to the user and allows the hand to be moved in different directions to scan for objects.

How I built the prototype

The prototype used a leather glove with a soft interior, an ultrasonic sensor, a small vibration motor, an Arduino Uno, a breadboard and jumper wires. I attached the sensor to the glove and placed the vibration motor near the thumb, while cushioning the sensitive electronic parts. A small cut in the glove allowed space for the breadboard and circuit, and an on/off button made the device easier to control. I chose a two-layer design to help protect the components while keeping the glove wearable.

The feedback was designed to communicate distance through different vibration patterns: one pulse for an object around 7–10 metres away, two pulses for 4–6 metres, and a closer warning for objects about 1–3 metres away. The report records a prototype weight of 425 grams and notes that reducing weight while retaining the required features was a significant design challenge.

Evaluation and learning

The project helped me understand that assistive technology must be judged by more than whether its electronics function. Comfort, grip, durability, ease of wearing, protection of the circuit and clarity of feedback all matter to the user. Further development would focus on reducing weight, testing detection reliability in different environments and refining the vibration patterns with user feedback. I learnt to translate a social need into a tangible prototype and to balance technical performance with usability.
