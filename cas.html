CAS · Summer 2026

Adventure, challenge & growth

Kitesurfing, scuba diving and skydiving pushed me to learn through experience: understanding the science behind each activity while building patience, confidence and respect for safety.

Kite surfing

Kite surfing turned my physics textbook into something I could feel. The kite taught me about lift and Bernoulli’s principle: air moving faster over the curved canopy creates low pressure, pulling the kite upward. Steering showed me vector resolution, since wind force splits into forward and sideways components. I understood Newton’s third law when the kite pulled me and the board pushed against the water. Friction and drag limited my speed, while centripetal force pulled me during sharp turns. Balancing taught me about torque and centre of gravity. Most importantly, I realised physics isn’t just equations; it’s the invisible force guiding every move.

Scuba diving

Scuba diving felt like stepping into another planet. The first lesson was buoyancy: Archimedes’ principle meant the water pushed up on me with a force equal to the weight of water I displaced, so adjusting my BCD (the inflatable vest) let me hover weightlessly. I learnt that pressure rises with depth, which is why my ears needed equalising every few metres. Colours faded as I descended because water absorbs red light first, turning everything blue-green. Breathing slowly through the regulator taught me calmness, since panic wastes air. Watching fish drift past, I felt tiny yet connected to something enormous. Scuba taught me patience, trust in my buddy, and respect for the ocean.

Skydiving

Skydiving was the most thrilling lesson of my life, and physics was falling right alongside me. The moment I left the plane, gravity accelerated me at 9.8 m/s², but soon air resistance grew until it balanced my weight, and I reached terminal velocity, around 200 km/h. My instructor explained that spreading my arms increased drag, while a streamlined position made me fall faster. When the parachute opened, the larger surface area suddenly increased drag, slowing my descent to a gentle glide. Landing showed me momentum and impulse, as bending my knees increased the stopping time and softened the impact. Beyond science, I learnt courage, trust in training, and that fear shrinks once you take the leap.

CAS learning

Across these activities, I connected classroom physics to real situations and practised perseverance, managing nerves, following safety instructions, and trusting instructors and activity partners.
