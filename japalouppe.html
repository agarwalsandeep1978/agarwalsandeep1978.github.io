Japalouppe Equestrian Centre

Ten days at Japalouppe felt like a lifetime of adventure packed into a single summer. I arrived knowing nothing about horses, only that they were enormous and I was not.

Learning to understand horses

On the first morning, our instructor taught us to approach calmly from the side, so the horse could see us, and to let it sniff our hand before touching it. Grooming, feeding and cleaning the stables came before any riding, and slowly I understood that a horse is not a machine but a living creature with moods.

Building trust

My horse was a stubborn bay with a white patch on his forehead. For days he ignored me, until patience finally won his trust. I learnt to balance in the saddle, keep my heels down, hold the reins gently, and use my legs to ask him to walk, trot and canter. The first canter felt like flying.

The fall

On the final day, my horse fell ill, and I was given an unfamiliar one. He sensed my nervousness at once. Halfway through the ride, he bolted, and I was thrown onto the soft ground. My pride hurt more than my body.

Climbing back up, I understood that every fall is just the horse’s way of teaching you to rise stronger.

What stayed with me

The experience taught me patience, humility and responsibility. Riding was not simply about control or achievement; it depended on trust, calm communication and respect for another living being.
